# Agency-Grade Creative Frameworks — Skill Reference

A field guide to the named, step-driven methodologies used by the big network
agencies (5A: WPP, Omnicom, Publicis, IPG, Dentsu) and their creative shops.
Each framework is structured for direct conversion into an agent skill:
**Name | Origin | One-line | Steps/Components | Skill hook**.

This is the raw material for `skills/creative-brief`, `skills/creative-review`,
and a `creative-director` role. Not all of it needs building — the quick-reference
map at the bottom prioritizes the highest-leverage conversions.

---

## 1. Account Planning & The Creative Brief

### The Creative Brief (universal agency document)
- **Origin:** Account planning tradition (Stanley Pollitt at BMP + Stephen King at JWT, London, late 1960s).
- **One-line:** The tight, written contract between strategy and creative that turns insight into an inspirational task.
- **Standard components (every agency's variant includes these):**
  1. **Background / Why are we here?** — the business/marketing problem, not history.
  2. **Objective** — the single communications task (one job, not a list).
  3. **Target audience** — who they *really* are (behaviors, attitudes, not just demographics).
  4. **Single-Minded Proposition (SMP) / Key Thought / "The One Thing"** — the one sentence creatives must dramatize. The hardest line on the brief.
  5. **Support / Reason to Believe (RTB)** — the facts that make the SMP credible.
  6. **Tone & Mandatories** — voice; what must/must not be included.
- **Skill hook:** A "brief-audit" checklist — every brief must contain objective + audience + SMP + RTB or it fails.

### BBDO — "GET / WHO / TO / BY" (GWTB)
- **Origin:** BBDO Worldwide (codified ~2004–2005; widely adopted network-wide).
- **One-line:** A one-line brief that compresses the entire strategy into four slots.
- **Format:** `GET [the audience] WHO [currently think/do X] TO [think/do Y] BY [the core message/idea].`
  - **GET** = target consumer (a real person, not a demo).
  - **WHO** = their current behavior/belief (the from-state).
  - **TO** = the desired response (the to-state).
  - **BY** = the single message / the lever.
- **Skill hook:** Auto-draft a GWTB line from any input; reject any brief where all four slots aren't filled.

### Ogilvy — "The big ideaL" (Big Ideal)
- **Origin:** Ogilvy & Mather; evolved out of the 360° Brand Steward approach.
- **One-line:** A brand purpose found at the intersection of a cultural tension and the brand's "best self."
- **The two axes:**
  - **Cultural Tension** — a conflict in the culture the brand could legitimately weigh in on.
  - **Brand Credibility / "Best Self"** — the space where the brand's skills/personality are believable.
- **The output statement:** *"The world would be a better place if…"* (e.g., Coca-Cola → if people saw the glass half full; adidas → if failure were seen as essential to success).
- **Skill hook:** Generate candidate Big Ideals by crossing a list of category tensions with a list of brand truths; score each for credibility.

### Saatchi & Saatchi / M&C Saatchi — "Brutal Simplicity of Thought"
- **Origin:** Maurice Saatchi; M&C Saatchi's founding doctrine.
- **One-line:** Reduce the strategy to one brutally simple thought that changes the world, then express it as simply as possible.
- **The test:** Can you state the idea in a single, undeniable sentence? If it takes two, it isn't simple enough.
- **Skill hook:** A "simplicity gate" — rewrite any idea/sentence in fewer words; reject multi-clause propositions.

### Wieden+Kennedy (W+K) Brief
- **Origin:** Wieden+Kennedy (Dan Wieden).
- **One-line:** A short, human, jargon-free brief written to inspire creatives, not to please clients.
- **Five sections:**
  1. **Background** — the current cultural/market moment (snapshot, not history).
  2. **Who are we talking to?** — real humans with desires, fears, motivations.
  3. **What do we want them to think/feel/do?** — human response, not business KPIs.
  4. **What's the insight?** — the unspoken human truth ("what they believe but haven't articulated").
  5. **What's the idea?** — simple, memorable, extendable.
- **Skill hook:** A "de-jargonizer" that flags marketing-speak and forces shorter, more human phrasing.

## 2. Ideation Frameworks (named creative methods)

### TBWA — "Disruption®" (the 3-step)
- **Origin:** Jean-Marie Dru, TBWA Worldwide, 1992.
- **One-line:** The antidote to incrementalism — break a convention to reach a bigger vision.
- **The three steps:**
  1. **Convention** — map the rules, habits, and "the way it's done" in the category.
  2. **Vision** — define a radically better future state for the brand/market.
  3. **Disruption** — invent the idea/action that breaks the convention and accelerates the move from #1 to #2.
- **Skill hook:** Run the Convention→Vision→Disruption sequence as a forced-output prompt chain.

### DDB — "R.O.I." (Relevance, Originality, Impact)
- **Origin:** Bill Bernbach / DDB, the Creative Revolution.
- **One-line:** Three qualities that separate great advertising from average — the creative yardstick.
- **The three criteria:**
  - **Relevance** — the message connects to the audience's real needs (otherwise originality is wasted).
  - **Originality** — it's genuinely new/divergent for the category (not a copy).
  - **Impact** — it's memorable and emotionally resonant; it sticks.
- **Note:** A "good on two, weak on one" idea is fixable; "good on one" gets killed.
- **Skill hook:** Score any idea 1–5 on each of R, O, I; reject anything scoring <3 on Relevance regardless of originality.

### Leo Burnett — "HumanKind" (acts, not ads)
- **Origin:** Leo Burnett Worldwide (the *HumanKind* book).
- **One-line:** Shift from making ads to creating acts that change human behavior, grounded in a human purpose.
- **The creation process (5 acts):**
  1. **Explore the human journey** — feel the behavior; don't just intellectualize it.
  2. **Inspire purpose** — find a human brand purpose (not a positioning statement).
  3. **Create acts, not just ads** — build platforms/experiences across touchpoints.
  4. **Activate & amplify purpose** — push the purpose into the world.
  5. **Measure transformation** — set metrics even when the client doesn't ask.
- **Skill hook:** Ask "is this an act or an ad?" as a filter; require a human-purpose statement before concepting.

### Droga5 — "Add value, not noise"
- **Origin:** David Droga / Droga5.
- **One-line:** World-class work comes from rigorous, measurable creative strategy rooted in brand purpose — "things people want and welcome," not noise.
- **Skill hook:** A "value-not-noise" test — does this idea earn the audience's attention, or interrupt it?

### "What If?" ideation
- **One-line:** A divergence technique that generates scenarios by asking "What if [constraint inversion]?"
- **How to run:** Take a category assumption, invert it into a "What if…?" question, generate 10+ answers. (assumption: "luxury is exclusive" → "What if luxury were radically inclusive?")
- **Skill hook:** Mechanical "What if" generator over a list of named category assumptions.

## 3. The Big Creative Test / Stress-Test Checklists

### The "On-Strategy" check (universal agency gate)
- **One-line:** Before presenting, every idea must answer a set of binary questions.
- **Checklist:**
  - Does it deliver the **Single-Minded Proposition**?
  - Is the **target audience** obviously the hero/subject?
  - Does it have a **reason to believe** baked in?
  - Is it **ownable** — could a competitor run it? (If yes, reject.)
  - Is it **single-minded** — one idea, or two glued together?
  - Does it pass the **"would the client buy it"** gut check?

### D&AD "three criteria"
- A good **idea**, well **executed**, **relevant** to the brief. Miss any one, no Pencil.

## 4. Positioning Frameworks (ad-world origin)

### Ries & Trout — "Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind"
- **Origin:** Al Ries & Jack Trout (1981 book; 1970s Industrial Marketing articles).
- **One-line:** Positioning is not what you do to the product; it's what you do to the mind of the prospect.
- **Core steps:**
  1. Find a **hole/position in the mind** that isn't yet owned.
  2. Be **first** in the mind (the leader strategy) — being first is more powerful than being better.
  3. If you can't be first, **create a new category you can be first in**.
  4. **Reposition the competition** — mentally re-slot the leader so a gap opens for you.
  5. Align your message with **perceptions already in the mind**, not with product facts.
- **Skill hook:** A positioning generator: identify owned words in the category, then find the unclaimed one.

### Stanley Pollitt — Account Planning (BMP)
- **Origin:** Stanley Pollitt, Boase Massimi Pollitt (BMP), London, 1968.
- **One-line:** Inject consumer research and strategy directly into creative development via a dedicated "planner" role.
- **Skill hook:** A "planner mode" that always asks "what does the consumer actually think/feel?" before any creative output.

### Saatchi & Saatchi — "One-Word Equity" (Maurice Saatchi)
- **One-line:** In the digital age, own a single word in the consumer's mind (Volvo = safety).
- **Skill hook:** A "one-word" distiller — given a brand, propose the single word it should own and stress-test whether a competitor already owns it.

### Saatchi & Saatchi — "Lovemarks" (Kevin Roberts)
- **One-line:** Move brands beyond "respect" (functional) into "love" (irrational loyalty) via a Love/Respect axis.
- **The three ingredients:**
  - **Mystery** — the stories, past, future, dreams behind the brand.
  - **Sensuality** — sound, sight, smell, touch, taste — the sensory experience.
  - **Intimacy** — empathy, commitment, passion, two-way connection.
- **The quadrant (Love × Respect):** low/low = commodity; high respect/low love = brands; low respect/high love = fads; **high love + high respect = Lovemarks**.
- **Skill hook:** Score a brand 1–10 on Mystery/Sensuality/Intimacy + Love/Respect; output its quadrant position.

## 5. Award / Effectiveness Rubrics (what judging actually rewards)

### Effie Awards — Marketing Effectiveness Framework (the gold standard for *results*)
- **Origin:** Effie Worldwide (American Marketing Association origin, 1968).
- **One-line:** Judged on whether the work *worked*, not just whether it was creative.
- **Round One scoring weights (official US percentages):**
  - **Challenge, Context & Objectives** — 23.3%
  - **Insights & Strategic Approach** — 23.3%
  - **Bringing the Strategy & Idea to Life** — 23.3%
  - **Results** — **30%** (results are weighted heaviest)
- **Case structure:** Challenge → Insight → Big Idea → Bringing Idea to Life → Results.
- **Skill hook:** Score any campaign output on the four Effie pillars; flag any concept with no measurable result hypothesis.

### Cannes Lions — Creative criteria
- **One-line:** The preeminent *creativity* award; scored 1–9 by jurors.
- **Creative Effectiveness Lions:** 25% **idea** / 25% **strategy** / 50% **impact & results**.

### Binet & Field — "The Long and the Short of It" (the effectiveness rulebook)
- **Origin:** Les Binet & Peter Field, IPA, based on the IPA datalink (~1,000 campaigns).
- **The headline rules:**
  - **60:40 rule** — split ~**60% to brand building / 40% to activation** for optimal long-term growth.
  - **95:5 rule** — at any moment only ~5% of B2B buyers are in-market; brand building is for the 95% out-of-market.
  - Emotion-driven brand campaigns outperform rational activation for long-term profit; pure short-term optimization *destroys* long-term growth.
- **Skill hook:** A budget-mode split checker — flag any plan that over-indexes (>50%) on activation as a long-term risk.
